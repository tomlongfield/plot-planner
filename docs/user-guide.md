# User guide

The plot planner keeps your growing plan in your browser. There's no account and nothing to install, though you can add it to your home screen.

## Getting started

1. **Look round the example.** The first time you open the planner it shows an example plot. Step through the months, select beds, and open a few plantings.
2. **Set your frost dates.** In **Settings → Frost dates**, choose your hemisphere and your average last spring frost and first autumn frost. Every sowing and planting date is worked out from these two dates. Ask at your allotment site or a garden centre, or look up a frost-date map for your country. If unsure, a later last frost and an earlier first frost are safer.
3. **Add your plot.** In **Settings → Plots → Add a plot**, give its size and say which compass direction is at the top of the plan. Draw it the way you picture it. When you're ready, delete the example (or keep it to refer to).
4. **Draw your beds.** In **Layout**, add beds and drag them into place, or type the numbers, which is easier on a phone. Drag a corner to resize. Split a bed into sections along its length, or divide a section into rows for interplanting. Add fences, paths, sheds, water butts and compost bays.
5. **Add plantings.** Choose a crop and variety, where it goes and how you'll raise it. The dates are worked out for you. Change any date and it stays as you set it.

### Looking things up (optional)

You never have to go online, but two lookups can save time. Each happens only when you press its button or tick its box.

- **Find a place** (Settings → Site) searches a town name to fill in your location. It sends only the words you type.
- **Look up for me** (Settings → Frost dates) works out your frost dates from ten years of local daily temperatures. You see the *safer* dates (frost-free in nine years out of ten) and the *typical* ones, with where they came from, and choose before anything changes. It sends only your coordinates.
- **Weather forecast** (Settings → Weather forecast) is off until you turn it on. Then the month view warns of frost when tender crops are out, dry spells, strong wind and heat.

All three use [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/), which is free and needs no account.

## The month view

- Each area is coloured by what is growing that month, with its name and a line or two about what's happening.
- A **solid green outline** marks areas where something changes that month: a planting goes in, harvest starts, or the bed is cleared.
- A **dashed outline and the word "draft"** marks drafts.
- **On the plot** lists jobs at the plot. **Elsewhere** lists jobs at home, in the propagator, and admin like ordering seed.
- Use the arrow keys on the month tabs to step through the months. **All months** shows the whole season as small plans; select one to open it.
- Select an area to see what's there now, what comes next and what grew there before.
- Change which months show in **Settings → Planning**.

## Plantings

A planting is a variety, in a place, over a date range. When you add one, the planner uses the crop's timing rules, your frost dates and any protection (a polytunnel, say) to work out when to sow, pot on, harden off, plant out, harvest and clear.

- **Successions:** set **Follows** to the crop before it in the same bed. If that crop runs late, the next one moves back, and plugs raised in modules show how long they're waiting.
- **Interplanting:** in a bed divided into rows, give each planting its rows. Or tick **Interplanted**.
- **Warnings** appear on the planting and in the plantings list: too many plants for the space, crops overlapping, the same family back too soon, a crop that needs a longer season than you have, plants left too long in modules, seed crops that will cross, and plantings that run past your plot's end date.
- **Progress:** tick off jobs, or use **Mark sown** and the other buttons, to record what actually happened.

## Drafts

Set a planting or task to **Draft** to try something without changing your plan. Plans other people share with you arrive as drafts too. Drafts show dashed on the plan. In **Plantings**, accept the drafts you want, or discard them.

## Tasks

The planner lists the jobs each planting needs. Add your own too, such as pruning or turning the compost, and make them repeat. Tick a job off with one tap.

**Add to calendar** saves a calendar file (.ics) that your phone or computer calendar can import, for reminders without an account. Save it again after big changes.

## Seeds

Record packets of seed, and sets, tubers, crowns and plants. The planner warns when seed is past its use-by date or older than it usually keeps.

**Import from spreadsheet** reads CSV or Excel (.xlsx) files. Match each column to what it holds, then check the rows. The planner suggests fixes for misspelt crop names, crops it doesn't know, plant families that don't match, duplicates and dates it can't read. Nothing is added until you choose **Add**.

## The crop library

**Crops** lists every crop with its timing in plain words and your next dates. Edit any value to suit your conditions: your edits are kept even when the library is updated. Add your own crops and varieties, and save a crop as a file to share it.

## Backing up

Your plan lives only in this browser, on this device. If you clear your browsing data, or the browser runs short of space, it could be lost.

- **Settings → Your data → Back up now** saves everything as one JSON file. Keep it somewhere safe, such as cloud storage or email to yourself.
- **Restore from backup** replaces what's in the browser with a backup file.
- To use the planner on another device, back up on one and restore on the other.
- The planner reminds you if you haven't backed up for a month.

## Using it offline

After the first visit the planner works with no signal. On a phone, use your browser's **Add to Home Screen** to open it like an app.

## Privacy

There are no accounts, cookies, analytics or adverts. The planner makes no requests to other websites unless you use one of the optional lookups above. What you enter stays on your device unless you save a file and share it yourself.
